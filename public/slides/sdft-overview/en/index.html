Self-Distillation Enables Continual Learning
Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning (SDFT)

SDFT uses a demonstration-conditioned version of itself as teacher, turning expert demonstrations into on-policy signals. It learns new tasks while forgetting much less than SFT.

On-policy learning forgets less, but usually needs rewards

With demonstrations alone, the default is SFT, an off-policy method.

The core tension

  • Continual learning: acquire skills and knowledge without degrading existing capabilities. Deployed models currently keep their parameters fixed.
  • On-policy training (learning from the model's own samples) reduces forgetting relative to off-policy training (RL's Razor, Shenfeld et al. 2025; Chen et al. 2025) and improves generalization (Chu et al. 2025).
  • Most successful on-policy methods use reinforcement learning, requiring explicit rewards that many real-world settings lack.
  • Learning from demonstrations mainly uses SFT: training on a fixed expert distribution leads to compounding errors at inference (Ross et al. 2011) and catastrophic forgetting.

Question: How can we learn on-policy from demonstrations alone?

Existing approaches and their limits

  • Inverse RL（IRL）：Infer a reward, then run on-policy RL. Strong structural priors (maximum entropy, adversarial discriminators, preference pairs) are needed; otherwise the problem is ill-posed or too costly.
  • Context distillation (Bai et al. 2022; Snell et al. 2022): a model with extra context teaches itself without that context. Usually offline, trained on teacher samples, with fixed context (e.g., guidelines or few-shot examples).

What SDFT changes

  • Distillation is on-policy: the student trains on its own trajectories, letting the teacher correct errors where they arise.
  • Teacher context is a demonstration specific to each query, rather than a global prefix, expressing fine-grained task intent.
  • The pretrained model's in-context learning (ICL) replaces an explicit reward model.
  • Concurrent independent work proposed similar self-distillation: Zhao et al. 2026; Hübotter et al. 2026 ("RL via self-distillation").

Student mode

Sees only query $x$ and samples $y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)$. All training trajectories come from this mode: on-policy.

Teacher mode

The same model (EMA weights) also sees demonstration $c$, producing $\pi(\cdot\mid y_{\lt t},x,c)$ at each student-generated position.

Key measurement (§3.2)

KL to base: teacher 0.68 nats, SFT 1.26 nats, at the same new-task accuracy.

Notation

  • $\theta$: student parameters, the only ones that receive gradients. Their value at step $k$ is $\theta_k$, abbreviated $\pi_k\equiv\pi_{\theta_k}$ (used on the next two slides).
  • The teacher $\pi(\cdot\mid x,c)$ is a constant in the loss. In the derivation it shares the student's parameters $\theta_k$; in the implementation it uses EMA parameters $\phi$, i.e. $\pi_\phi(\cdot\mid x,c)$ (algorithm on the next slide).

Objective

Sample $y$ from the student and minimize student-to-teacher KL:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)\,\|\,\pi(\cdot\mid x,c)\big)=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}\left[\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi(y\mid x,c)}\right]$$(1)

Step 1: differentiate (1). $\theta$ appears in both the sampling distribution and the log ratio. The score-function identity $\nabla\pi_\theta=\pi_\theta\nabla\log\pi_\theta$ handles the former; the latter contributes $\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y\mid x)]=0$:

$$\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}\left[\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi(y\mid x,c)}\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y\mid x)\right]$$(2a)

Step 2: approximate per token. $\log\pi(y\mid x)=\sum_t\log\pi(y_t\mid y_{\lt t},x)$. At each position the paper keeps only that position's KL, summed exactly over the vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$ (the analytic per-token estimator):

$$g=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}\sum_t\sum_{y_t\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_\theta(y_t\mid y_{\lt t},x)\,\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_t\mid y_{\lt t},x)}{\pi(y_t\mid y_{\lt t},x,c)}\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_t\mid y_{\lt t},x)$$(2)

(2) is not (2a): it drops how $y_t$ changes the ratios of later tokens, which amounts to minimizing $\sum_t D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ on fixed student prefixes. This is the sequence-level bias noted in Appendix A.1.

  • Supervision is dense: every token, over the full vocabulary, from one rollout per query; GRPO-style methods give one advantage per trajectory and need a group of samples.
  • The theory uses reverse KL, but forward KL works best in practice; the repository README (2026-04-07) says all paper results use per-token forward KL.

Algorithm and key design choices

Each choice is either ablated in the appendix or used by default in the main experiments.

Algorithm 1 (SDFT)

Initialize teacher weights $\phi\leftarrow\theta$
For each training step:
Sample demonstration minibatch $\{(x_i,c_i)\}$
For each pair in parallel:
$y_i\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid \mathrm{Ctx}_S(x_i))$ — on-policy rollout
$\ell^S_{i,t}\leftarrow\log\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid y_{i,\lt t},\mathrm{Ctx}_S(x_i))$
$\ell^T_{i,t}\leftarrow\log\pi_\phi(y_{i,t}\mid y_{i,\lt t},\mathrm{Ctx}_T(x_i,c_i))$
$g\leftarrow$ analytic per-token KL gradient, averaged over $i$
Optionally use importance sampling for inference/training engine differences
$\theta\leftarrow\theta-\eta\,g$
$\phi\leftarrow\alpha\,\theta+(1-\alpha)\,\phi$ — EMA teacher

Teacher weights (Appendix A.3)

  • Frozen base model: stable but weaker; it cannot track the student's improvements.
  • Current student directly: unstable; small fluctuations are amplified by the on-policy loop, causing divergence.
  • EMA of student weights ($\alpha\in\{0.01,0.02,0.05\}$): tracks progress and smooths updates. Default choice.

KL gradient estimator (Appendix A.1)

  • Sampled token-level estimator：Higher variance and weaker KL control.
  • Analytic per-token estimator (sum over vocabulary at each step): biased at sequence level, but most stable and effective. Default choice.
  • Rao-Blackwellized estimator (unbiased): extra cost without measurable gains.
  • Multiple rollouts per query: negligible gains with much more compute.

Suppressing artifacts (§5)

Students may learn teacher phrases such as "Based on the text…". Masking loss on the first few tokens removes these without harming accuracy.

An inverse RL view: demonstrations define an implicit reward

Under an assumption, self-distillation is equivalent to on-policy RL with rewards supplied by the model's own ICL.

Derivation (§3.1)

  1. One trust-region RL update ($\pi_k=\pi_{\theta_k}$ is the current student; $\pi$ ranges over all policies):
    $$\pi_{k+1}=\arg\max_{\pi}\ \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi}\big[r(y,x)\big]-\beta\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi(\cdot\mid x)\,\|\,\pi_k(\cdot\mid x)\big)$$(3)
  2. The closed-form optimum is a tilted distribution $\pi^*_{k+1}(y\mid x)\propto\pi_k(y\mid x)\,e^{r(y,x)/\beta}$, hence
    $$r(y,x)=\beta\big[\log\pi^*_{k+1}(y\mid x)-\log\pi_k(y\mid x)\big]+C$$
  3. In-context assumption: The same model (parameters $\theta_k$), conditioned on the demonstration, approximates the unknown optimal policy
    $$\pi^*_{k+1}(y\mid x)\approx\pi(y\mid x,c)$$(4)
  4. Implicit reward (dropping $\beta$ and $C$) and its per-token form, which sums to the sequence reward:
    $$r(y,x,c)=\log\frac{\pi(y\mid x,c)}{\pi_k(y\mid x)},\qquad r_t=\log\frac{\pi(y_t\mid y_{\lt t},x,c)}{\pi_k(y_t\mid y_{\lt t},x)}$$(5)
  5. Policy gradient (evaluated at $\theta=\theta_k$, reward held fixed)
    $$\nabla_\theta J\big|_{\theta_k}=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_k}\left[\log\frac{\pi(y\mid x,c)}{\pi_k(y\mid x)}\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y\mid x)\big|_{\theta_k}\right]$$(6)
    This is exactly the negative of Equation (2a) at $\theta=\theta_k$: maximizing this reward minimizes reverse KL. The implemented Equation (2) is a per-token approximation of (2a).

Interpretation

  • The student is rewarded for approaching its more informed, demonstration-conditioned self.
  • No reward model or explicit reward prior is required: the prior is the pretrained model's ICL ability.
  • The trust-region term explains anchoring: the target is the high-reward policy closest to the current model. This starts the forgetting argument on the next two slides.

Caution

This equivalence concerns reverse KL. Experiments use forward KL, so the IRL argument motivates the method rather than exactly describing the implemented loss.

Why less forgetting? (I) The ideal target changes as little as needed

① The optimum's minimum-change property

"High reward, but not far from $\pi_k$" can be written two ways. Equation (3) puts a price $\beta$ on change. Alternatively, set a passing mark $v$ for reward and pick the passing policy that changes least:

$$\min_{\pi}\ D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi\,\|\,\pi_k\big)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi}\big[r(y,x)\big]\ge v$$

With $v=\mathbb{E}_{\pi^*_{k+1}}[r]$, the reward of Equation (3)'s optimum $\pi^*_{k+1}=\pi_k\,e^{r/\beta}/Z$ (normalizer $Z=\sum_y\pi_k(y\mid x)\,e^{r(y,x)/\beta}$), both give the same answer. Taking logs gives $\log\frac{\pi^*_{k+1}}{\pi_k}=\frac{r}{\beta}-\log Z$. For any $\pi$:

$$\begin{aligned}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi\,\|\,\pi_k)&=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\Big[\log\frac{\pi}{\pi_k}\Big]&&\text{definition of KL}\\&=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\Big[\log\frac{\pi}{\pi^*_{k+1}}\Big]+\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\Big[\log\frac{\pi^*_{k+1}}{\pi_k}\Big]&&\text{multiply and divide by }\pi^*_{k+1}\\&=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi\,\|\,\pi^*_{k+1})+\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\Big[\frac{r}{\beta}-\log Z\Big]&&\text{a KL; substitute the log ratio}\\&=\underbrace{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi\,\|\,\pi^*_{k+1})}_{\ge 0,\ =0\iff\pi=\pi^*_{k+1}}+\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[r]}{\beta}-\log Z&&Z\text{ does not depend on }y\end{aligned}$$

At $\pi=\pi^*_{k+1}$ the first term is 0, so $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi^*_{k+1}\,\|\,\pi_k)=v/\beta-\log Z$. A passing $\pi$ has $\mathbb{E}_\pi[r]\ge v$, so $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi\,\|\,\pi_k)\ge 0+v/\beta-\log Z=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi^*_{k+1}\,\|\,\pi_k)$, with equality only at $\pi=\pi^*_{k+1}$: among policies reaching the same reward, $\pi^*_{k+1}$ is closest to $\pi_k$ (the standard I-projection result).

①′ What the optimum implies

  • Reweights only outputs already supported by $\pi_k$: $\pi_k(y\mid x)=0\Rightarrow\pi^*_{k+1}(y\mid x)=0$.
  • Larger $\beta$ makes change pricier: a lower passing mark $v$ and a smaller change.

② Two conditions for the teacher (§3.2)

  • Optimality: teacher samples achieve near-maximal reward
    $$\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi(\cdot\mid x,c)}\big[r(y,x)\big]\approx\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi^*_{k+1}}\big[r(y,x)\big]$$
  • Minimal deviation: the teacher is as close to $\pi_k$ as the optimum
    $$D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi(\cdot\mid x,c)\,\|\,\pi_k\big)\approx D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi^*_{k+1}\,\|\,\pi_k\big)$$
  • The second condition supports retention: teacher and student share the same parameters and differ only in context, so outputs keep the model's style. A copying teacher would violate it.
  • ToolAlpaca measurement: teacher accuracy 100% (base 42%); KL to base 0.68 nats vs SFT 1.26, at the same new-task accuracy.

Why less forgetting? (II) Small KL, retention, and the gaps

The third link relies on prior empirical findings; the derivation also differs from actual training.

③ Empirical relation (RL's Razor and others)

Prior work (Shenfeld et al. 2025; Chen et al. 2025) finds forgetting increases with KL between the fine-tuned and base policies on new-task inputs,

$$\text{Forgetting}\ \uparrow\quad\text{as}\quad\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{\text{new}}}\,\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi_0(\cdot\mid x),\,\pi(\cdot\mid x)\big)\ \uparrow$$

and on-policy training tends to select smaller-KL solutions, whereas SFT does not.

This is a correlation, not a theorem; the paper cites it without re-deriving it.

Intuition: two gradients

SFT increases expert-token probability on expert prefixes:

$$\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{SFT}}=-\,\mathbb{E}_{(x,y^\star)\sim\mathcal{D}}\sum_t\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y^\star_t\mid y^\star_{\lt t},x)$$

When $\pi_\theta(y^\star_t\mid\cdot)$ is small, the gradient moves substantial probability mass; parameter changes can affect unrelated inputs too.

SDFT matches $\pi(\cdot\mid y_{\lt t},x,c)$ on student prefixes $y\sim\pi_\theta$: the target is already near the model's distribution, requiring less change.

Gaps in the argument

  • KL direction: Theory uses reverse KL; training uses per-token forward KL. The IRL argument does not cover the implemented loss.
  • Estimator：The analytic per-token estimator is biased at sequence level (Appendix A.1), another departure.
  • EMA teacher: The derivation assumes a teacher based on $\pi_k$; EMA lags the student, so the target is approximate.
  • Limited evidence: Minimal deviation rests on one task's numbers (0.68 vs 1.26), measured against $\pi_0$, not each step's $\pi_k$.
  • No old-task guarantee: KL is measured on new-task inputs; it gives no bound for other inputs.
  • Forgetting remains: After Science Q&A, IFEval falls from 74.3 to 66.8. The claim is reduced forgetting.

Experimental setup

Two kinds of post-training adaptation: acquiring skills and absorbing new facts.

Skill learning

Tasks the model was not specifically tuned for (unlike math and code).

  • Science Q&A：SciKnowEval Chemistry L-3, multiple choice, 75/5/20 split. GPT-4o demonstrations: retain one correct response from up to 8 samples per question.
  • Tool Use：ToolAlpaca, original split and demonstrations. Regex scoring of API calls, allowing argument-order variation.
  • Medical：HuatuoGPT-o1: about 20,000 English SFT examples for training; 1,000 verifiable questions for evaluation, graded by GPT-5-mini.

Knowledge acquisition

  • About 200,000 tokens of Wikipedia articles on 2025 natural disasters, after the model's cutoff (Myanmar and Kamchatka earthquakes, Hurricane Melissa, Central Texas floods, etc.).
  • GPT-5 generates question-answer pairs, about 5 times the source corpus size.
  • Metrics: strict (all details correct), lenient (correct information without errors), OOD: indirect questions requiring these facts without explicitly naming them (e.g., which countries needed international humanitarian aid in 2025?).

Prior capabilities

Average over HellaSwag, TruthfulQA, MMLU, IFEval, Winogrande, and HumanEval (lm-eval-harness), measuring forgetting.

Baselines

  • Skills: SFT; DFT (importance sampling treats offline data as on-policy samples); SFT + Re-invocation (on-policy distillation from base on generic prompts to restore capabilities).
  • Knowledge: continual pretraining (CPT) on articles; SFT on question-answer pairs; oracle RAG that always retrieves the correct article.

Protocol

Full fine-tuning with TRL on one H200. Tune learning rate, batch size, and epochs separately for each method; select the best validation checkpoint. Greedy decoding, 3 seeds. SDFT max generation length 2048 (skills) or 1024 (knowledge).

Discussion and limitations (authors' statements)

SDFT complements on-policy RL when demonstrations are available but rewards are not.

Relationship to RL

  • Different supervision: demonstrations without rewards, versus rewards optimized through exploration.
  • Can combine: initialize RL with SDFT (supported by pass@k gains), or mix both signals.
  • Cheaper per step than GRPO: one rollout per query, with token- or even logit-level credit assignment.

Costs and failure modes

  • Compute: About 2.5 times SFT FLOPs and 4 times wall-clock time. Total time may be lower than SFT plus a recovery stage (e.g., Re-invoke).
  • Artifacts: Students imitate phrases such as "Following the example…"; masking early tokens is a heuristic fix.
  • Strong ICL required: fails at 3B.
  • Small behavioral changes only: cannot teach explicit CoT to a non-reasoning model.
  • Residual forgetting (e.g., IFEval 74.3 → 66.8 after Science Q&A).

Future work

  • Combine with on-policy RL: SDFT before RL, or jointly mix demonstration and reward signals.
  • Further reduce residual forgetting.
  • Learn from non-expert or noisy demonstrations.
  • Learn from unstructured data (e.g., user conversations) for real-world continual learning.
§518

Summary: a demonstration-informed self supplies on-policy signals

These final two slides are our assessment, rather than the paper's statements.

Contribution

SDFT enables on-policy learning from demonstrations without rewards: on its own rollouts, the student distills from the same model (EMA weights) conditioned on demonstrations. On Qwen2.5-7B skill and knowledge tasks, it outperforms SFT on new tasks while largely retaining prior capabilities.

Findings to remember

  • Learn more, forget less: Tool Use 70.6 vs SFT 63.2; prior-task average 65.4 vs 56.0 (base 65.5).
  • Knowledge integration: OOD accuracy on injected facts 98 vs SFT 80, near oracle RAG's 100.
  • Answer-only reasoning adaptation: Olmo-3-7B-Think Medical 43.7 vs SFT 23.5; response length retains 91%.
  • On-policy is key: Offline distillation from the same teacher stays below SDFT (Figure 6).
  • ICL matters: SDFT trails SFT by 3.3 at 3B and leads by 6.9 at 14B.

Convincing evidence

  • Consistent reduction in forgetting: 3 tasks × 6 prior benchmarks; SDFT's prior-task average loses at most 1.0 vs base. TruthfulQA drops 7.7–11.1 for SFT/DFT, at most 1.4 for SDFT (Table 5).
  • Mechanism isolated: Figure 6 fixes the teacher and changes the training distribution, attributing gains to on-policy learning beyond teacher quality.
  • Ablated design choices: Teacher context (Figure 7), teacher weights (Figure 8), and KL estimator (Appendix A.1) have separate controls.
  • Beyond sharper outputs: pass@k consistently leads for $k\le 128$ (Figure 5, right).
  • Three sequential tasks show cumulative learning consistent with single-task results (Figure 3).

Critique: theory differs from training; broader transfer is unproven

Each point cites a specific section of the paper.

Thin evidence

  • Theory does not cover the actual loss: §3.1's IRL equivalence uses reverse KL, but all results use per-token forward KL (README, 2026-04-07); the analytic estimator is sequence-level biased (Appendix A.1).
  • One minimal-deviation measurement: 0.68 vs 1.26 nats, one task, against $\pi_0$ rather than each step's $\pi_k$ (§3.2).
  • Narrow scope: Mostly Qwen2.5-7B; scaling only on Science Q&A; only one reasoning model, Olmo-3-7B-Think.
  • Unmatched compute: SDFT uses about 2.5 times the FLOPs and 2–4 epochs; no compute-matched SFT or RL control.
  • Uncertainty not shown: Appendix B.2 says 95% CI over 3 seeds, but Tables 1, 2, 5 omit intervals.
  • Grading and retention metrics: Medical and knowledge tasks use GPT-5-mini grading; the prior-capability suite lacks math or long-reasoning benchmarks.

Open questions

  • Would reverse KL (matching the theory) give the same results? No direct forward/reverse comparison is reported.
  • At equal FLOPs, how does SDFT compare with more SFT epochs or RL when rewards exist?
  • Can the teacher prompt change a thinking model's reasoning style and distill that change into the student?
  • Does retention hold above 30B, across model families, and over longer task sequences?
  • With noisy or weak-model demonstrations, does the teacher still meet the optimality condition?
